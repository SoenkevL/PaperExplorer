The eval does not meet its bar, so point 4 of the asking work's definition of done is not reached. hit@8 is the forced search alone, the same in both runs. No exact Quote in either run, in either mode (14 in all), differs from the printed text at its place: exactness by construction holds.

**How the Answers were judged** (the verdicts files say it too). *Grounded*: every claim is borne out by the Seed, by the place a Pointer leads to, else by the Chunks the model read (all of them, in the baseline); one claim the Seed does not make or contradicts makes the Answer ungrounded. A true sentence without a Pointer is not held against it, since Pointer coverage measures that. *Correct*: the substance on the point asked is right; an invented or wrong statement on that point, or an Answer that misses it, is not. A failed turn is neither. Read strictly, so that a grounded Answer must also point every substantive sentence (uncovered Questions excepted), retrieval scores 7 of 22 in both runs and the baseline 7 (run 1) and 12 (run 2).

## The Support threshold

`PE_SUPPORT_THRESHOLD` stays **0.70**, now set from the eval rather than provisional.

Every Pointer of both runs was read against the Seed (72 in run 1, 116 in run 2). Does the place it leads to bear its sentence out? *Apt*: the place says what the sentence says. *Partly*: it bears out part of the sentence (typically one item of a list sentence), or a nearby sentence would do better. *Not*: it does not bear the sentence out. Each reading is in the verdicts files, per Pointer.

| Cosine | Run 1: apt / partly / not | Run 2: apt / partly / not | Both |
|---|---|---|---|
| ≥ 0.75 | 44 / 1 / 0 | 72 / 12 / 0 | 129, none inapt |
| 0.70–0.75 | 9 / 6 / 0 | 8 / 14 / 2 | 39, 2 inapt |
| 0.60–0.70 | 1 / 5 / 2 | 1 / 2 / 0 | 11, 2 inapt |
| < 0.60 | 2 / 1 / 1 | 0 / 1 / 4 | 9, 5 inapt |

- Of 168 Pointers at or above 0.70, 2 are inapt. Both belong to one sentence carrying an invented figure (run 2 baseline `79cfebddd42f39f85acaeeeb31294922`). A cosine measures how near the topic is, not whether the claim is true, so no threshold catches them.
- Below 0.70, 7 of 20 are inapt, and most of the rest bear out one item of a list sentence: weak is the right word for those.
- Raising the threshold to 0.75 would make the 37 apt or partly apt Pointers of the 0.70–0.75 band weak, to catch those 2. Lowering it to 0.65 would call an inapt Pointer close (run 1 `shortcoming-fmri` retrieval, 0.68).
- Close and weak separate no more cleanly anywhere between 0.60 and 0.75. The 0.70–0.75 band is more often partly apt than apt, so close there means "a right place, perhaps for part of the sentence". The sample is small (188 Pointers, 9 inapt), so future runs should read their Pointers the same way.

## Failure patterns

Each pattern names the traces that show it (Langfuse, `eval` environment).

1. **The model calls tools on a request that offers none.** This is the root cause of every failed turn in run 1, and it is fixed.
   - In retrieval, all 8 turns that reached the sixth request failed there with a tool call: `0ba24102b1727f29db6f883add6e79a3`, `28d3ebb52f7953b5af83089e763d8834`, `64faeb3281ab0752535be216ae18d0bf`, `a212d4b779a3aac9b5c224b96b0ff5c7`, `1d35e176a2e24c6088b6026406cfc61d`, `c622d53f92c5277ee84830a0f5ee849b`, `3c35c29301d8576af4eac06ee1002271`, `0d1324a9fce7818f75103fcf9948038a`.
   - Their traces show the sixth request offering no tools (empty function tools) and ending in `tool_call`. pydantic-ai answers such a call with a retry prompt, and the seventh request that would need trips the limit.
   - The note saying it was the last request was a third system message at the top, ahead of up to 17k tokens of reading. Changing the top of the prompt also emptied Ollama's prompt cache: the sixth request took 6–14 s before its first byte, against 0.5–4 s for the earlier ones.
   - In the baseline, which offers no tools at all, 3 of 22 turns called tools that do not exist: `Read` (`d87f1f36136df123414ad8f4a41a7a2b`), `read_section`, `search` and `read` (`cbd486a7114fdbc580ab8e9f4b397a75`), and `read_range`, `get_map` and `read_all` (`356df10f4a9dce19597d1ddeddd037aa`). Two failed at pydantic-ai's second call to the same unknown tool; the third stalled (pattern 3).
   - Replayed on the tower from the recorded traces, three times each, the 8 sixth requests were answered 1 time in 24 as sent. With the note after the last tool result they were answered 20 times in 24 as a plain user turn, and 23 in 24 as a `<system>` turn, the way pydantic-ai renders a mid-conversation system part. The 3 failed baseline requests were answered 5 times in 9 as sent and 9 in 9 with their note last.
   - Fix (in `asking`, regression tests from these traces): every request without tools ends with its note, for that request only. **Run 2:** no turn failed; all 10 retrieval turns that reached the sixth request answered on it; the baseline made no tool calls.
2. **Reading instead of answering.** The model spends its requests on tools even when the forced search already answers. In run 2, 10 of 22 retrieval turns used all six requests, among them two uncovered Questions whose "not covered" Answer needed none of the reading (`743e05e0a4b14b7d4714b08066fb1a44`, `98818168b37761c68108a3d4b0ac969b`). Those turns took 48–78 s, against 13–21 s for turns answered on the first request. Its thinking can also drift: in `0ba24102b1727f29db6f883add6e79a3` it reasoned about a follow-up Question the reader never asked.
3. **A stream that stalls** (`356df10f4a9dce19597d1ddeddd037aa`, run 1 baseline). After three calls to unknown tools, the fourth request streamed the start of the model's thinking, then nothing until the OpenAI client's 600 s timeout. Most likely Ollama held back a tool call the model never finished; its log was not readable to confirm. Fix: chat requests time out after 120 s without bytes (`PE_CHAT_TIMEOUT_S`). Run 2 had no stall, so the timeout was not exercised there.
4. **Tools that do not exist, mid-turn.** Retrieval named a tool that does not exist five times: `Gemma2::respond` (`1d35e176a2e24c6088b6026406cfc61d`) and `cite` (`0d1324a9fce7818f75103fcf9948038a`) in run 1, `mezzanine` (`7ac7a610d43186032e5190d1da34a2c2`), `read_headings` (`69f81fddcda7adc1a3e8d0c4f11ba5f0`) and `read_content` (`81169eb5ba715db19037ac3ccfebec3e`) in run 2. Each time pydantic-ai's retry prompt named the real tools and the model went on. This is left as it is: it costs one request.
5. **Handles in the wrong form, or none.** This is most of the missing Pointer coverage.
   - In run 1, three covered retrieval Answers carry 22 of the 23 unpointed sentences: no handles in `49f2a82d3fa6f54df59cc780a76aaa08`, an invented form in `6db73737200381fe360218af04d45075`, reference-style numbers in `94b4ec34a550a419d156bf3befa3dd40`. The other Answers point 24 of their 25 sentences.
   - In run 2, four carry 20 of 34: no handles in `d67515e0ace2d64ba5621fb736d7e883`, `dc6e78946bae1373b0ebe16ded825235` and `85b49b1e445ad5402eff89fe68849ded`; handles in parentheses in `c4718fba6b318aa28f6a4e0e8f79d68b`.
   - The baseline writes `[11]`, `(#95)`, `{#92}`, `[#59##60]` and `()` (for example `c424915d193fc9be73521dcfd0e2b217`, `1598bd4a15cc3a832762d1d25f1e00fb`, `8317ea8cc76746304bee75a9f9cc5a64`, `83a88c01e395c5c494da1bd5bcba73d7`).
   - So coverage is nearly all or nothing per Answer.
6. **Claims the Seed does not make** (the ungrounded verdicts).

7. **Leaked template tokens and monologue.**

## What would raise the failing numbers

- **Pointer coverage (0.55).** The missing Pointers sit in a few Answers that write no handles or the wrong form (pattern 5).
  - Two changes would act on it. First, read every form that carries a `#` as handles, so `(#5)`, `{#5}`, `<#5>`, `[#5–#7]` and `[#5, 6]` count; `[5]` must stay the Seed's own marker. Second, when a covered Answer has substantive sentences and no handle, and requests remain, ask once more for the handles.
  - In run 2 the first would have recovered one of the four handle-less Answers (4 sentences, to 0.61), the second the other three (16 sentences, to about 0.76 if each is then pointed), both together about 0.82. Both change `asking`'s design (ADR 0008's handle grammar, or one more request), so they are the user's call.
  - Dropping the leaked lines of pattern 7 would also help the baseline.
- **Judged grounded and correct (68%).** The misses are invented or reversed claims (pattern 6) and missed points (pattern 8), not retrieval: hit@8 is 1.00 and Pointers sit on expected places 82% of the time. Three levers, each to measure with the eval:
  - a lower sampling temperature, since the Modelfile sets none;
  - a model that follows instructions more literally than this agentic fine-tune: it calls tools nobody offered, invents tool names and handle forms, and leaks its template;
  - less reading per turn (pattern 2), since the baseline, with the whole Seed and one request, is judged right 86% of the time.
- **Retrieval or the whole Seed.** For a Seed this size, the whole Seed in context is better and faster with this model. Answering from the whole Seed whenever it fits the context, and searching only beyond it (Sources), would change the agent's design (ADR 0009, `design-asking.md`). That is the user's call.
- **Leaked tokens (pattern 7).** Stripping the chat template's control tokens from the draft, and failing a turn whose Answer has no words left, would stop the reader seeing them. It is small, but what counts as a control token, and whether to fix it in the Modelfile's template instead, is a decision.
